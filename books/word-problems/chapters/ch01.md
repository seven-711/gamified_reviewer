# Chapter 1: Number Problems & The 7-Step Strategy

## Learning Objectives
1. Master the 7-step word problem decomposition technique used in CSE and CET exams.
2. Formulate "Let" statements by assigning $x$ to the base (smaller) quantity.
3. Use visual tape diagrams (bar modeling) to translate word relationships into linear equations without guessing.
4. Solve sum problems and consecutive integer problems step-by-step.

## Beat Sequence & Storyboard

### Beat 1: `intro`
- **Visual**: Unit 1, Chapter 1 header card. Title "Number Problems & The 7-Step Strategy".
- **Narration**: "Unit one, chapter one. [[start]]Word problems are not mysteries; they are puzzles waiting for a picture."
- **Action**: Fade in title, slide subtitle into focus, wipe to main stage.

### Beat 2: `strategy`
- **Visual**: A 3-step action card appears: (1) Find the target question, (2) Let $x$ be the baseline unknown, (3) Build the picture.
- **Narration**: "Every word problem [[target]]ends with a target question. Always let [[xvar]]x represent the smaller baseline quantity, and [[sketch]]draw the relationship before writing equations."

### Beat 3: `problem`
- **Visual**: Problem banner card enters: "Two numbers have a sum of 72. One number is twice the other. What are the numbers?"
- **Narration**: "Here is a classic exam question: [[prompt]]Two numbers have a sum of 72. One number is twice the other. What are the numbers?"

### Beat 4: `bars`
- **Visual**: Tape diagram renders on stage.
  - Row 1: Green block labelled $x$ (Smaller number).
  - Row 2: Two equal green blocks labelled $x$ and $x$ ($2x$, Larger number).
  - Dimension bracket across both totaling 72.
- **Narration**: "Instead of guessing, [[draw]]draw tape bars. One bar for the smaller number, and [[double]]two identical bars for the larger number. Together, their total [[bracket]]sum is 72."

### Beat 5: `solve`
- **Visual**: The 3 blocks line up: $x + 2x = 3x$. The bracket shows $3x = 72$. Dividing 72 into 3 slots gives 24 per block. Smaller = 24, Larger = 48.
- **Narration**: "Notice what the picture tells us: [[combine]]three identical parts equal 72. Dividing 72 by 3 [[divide]]gives 24 for each block. So the smaller number is [[ans1]]24, and the larger number is [[ans2]]48."

### Beat 6: `q1` (Quick check 1)
- **Interactive Check**: Position `BAND`.
  - Prompt: "Two numbers sum to 45. One number is twice the other. What is the smaller number?"
  - Type: `blanks` (`x = 15`).
  - Hint: "3 equal parts sum to 45. Divide 45 by 3."

### Beat 7: `consecutive`
- **Visual**: Consecutive numbers diagram.
  - Block 1: Length $x$.
  - Block 2: Length $x$ plus an orange unit block $+1$.
  - Equation line: $x + (x + 1) = 2x + 1$.
- **Narration**: "When problems mention [[consec]]consecutive integers, the second number is simply [[plusone]]x plus 1. Their sum creates two equal blocks, plus a single [[unit]]unit."

### Beat 8: `q2` (Quick check 2)
- **Interactive Check**: Position `BAND`.
  - Prompt: "The sum of two consecutive integers is 35. What is the smaller integer?"
  - Type: `choice`. Options: 17, 18, 16. Correct: 17 ($2x + 1 = 35 \implies 2x = 34 \implies x = 17$).

### Beat 9: `wrap`
- **Visual**: 3 numbered takeaway plaques.
  1. Let $x$ represent the smaller unknown.
  2. Tape diagrams reveal the total number of equal parts.
  3. Consecutive numbers increase by $+1$ for each step.
- **Narration**: "Remember three key rules: [[w1]]First, let x be your smaller base unknown. [[w2]]Second, bar models make the equal parts obvious. [[w3]]Third, consecutive numbers add one for each step."

### Beat 10: `final1`
- **Full-Screen Practice**: Position `SCREEN`.
  - Question: "The sum of three consecutive integers is 48. What is the largest integer?"
  - Type: `choice`. Options: [15, 16, 17, 18]. Correct: 17 ($x + (x+1) + (x+2) = 48 \implies 3x + 3 = 48 \implies 3x = 45 \implies x = 15$; largest is $15 + 2 = 17$).

- **Score Card**: `finishCard` displaying accuracy on first attempts.
